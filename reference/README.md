# Reference and Further Reading

What this library is, what it draws on, and where to go if you want the underlying research. Read this if
you are wondering whether you can share the pack, or if you want the primary sources behind an idea.

---

## This pack is an original synthesis

Every document here was written for this library. Nothing in it reproduces text from any third-party book,
course, or paid publication. Ideas from other people's work are re-expressed in operational wording —
what tends to happen, under what conditions, and what to do about it — and the schools of thought are
credited below by name.

Three constraints held throughout:

- **No third-party text is reproduced.** Where an idea originates elsewhere, it is restated, not quoted.
- **No invented evidence.** No research finding is expressed as a percentage, a conversion-lift figure, a
  study citation, or a sample testimonial. Research findings are described qualitatively, because a number
  nobody can check is worse than no number at all. Numbers that do appear are settings and arithmetic — a
  saturation value, a contrast ratio, a sample-size formula — not claims about the world.
- **Manipulation is named.** Where a technique works by exploiting confusion rather than by helping someone
  decide, the relevant document says so and marks the line.

## Nothing paid is redistributed here

No proprietary, licensed, or purchased material is bundled in this repository. If a body of work below
interests you, buy it from the people who made it. That is both the honest position and the useful one — the
primary sources carry the experimental detail, the caveats, and the citations that a synthesis necessarily
compresses.

## The bodies of work this draws on

- **Consumer psychology, applied to marketing and interfaces** — notably the published writing of Nick
  Kolenda on how people process, price, and decide. The strongest debt in this pack is the framing of
  processing ease, association spillover, and mental simulation as the mechanisms most tactics reduce to.
- **Influence and persuasion research** — the tradition associated with Robert Cialdini: reciprocity, social
  proof, authority, commitment, liking, scarcity, and the conditions under which each holds or backfires.
- **Positioning and brand strategy** — the classic literature, including the positioning tradition of Ries
  and Trout and the brand-equity work of David Aaker, along with the jobs-to-be-done framing associated with
  Clayton Christensen and Bob Moesta.
- **Behavioural economics and judgement under uncertainty** — anchoring, framing, loss aversion, and the
  limits of each, from the Kahneman and Tversky line of research and its successors.
- **Usability and interaction design** — the discount-usability tradition associated with Jakob Nielsen and
  Don Norman, including heuristic evaluation, small-sample usability testing, and affordance and feedback as
  design obligations.
- **Direct-response copywriting** — the specificity-and-mechanism school running from Claude Hopkins through
  the modern direct-response writers: concrete claims, stated reasons, testable promises.
- **Web accessibility and performance guidance** — the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines and the
  widely-adopted user-centric performance metrics, which supply the contrast ratios, keyboard requirements,
  and loading targets used in the build and launch documents.
- **Current search and AI-search practice** — search-engine documentation on structured data, indexing, and
  crawling, alongside the emerging conventions for making content quotable by AI answer engines.
- **Statistics for experimentation** — standard treatments of power, minimum detectable effect, and stopping
  rules, which supply the arithmetic in the measurement document.

## How to read further

Start with the primary sources for the ideas you intend to lean on hardest. If your brand's problem is
belief, read the influence and copywriting sources. If it is comprehension, read the usability sources. If it
is being found, read the search engines' own documentation before anything written about it.

Two cautions worth carrying into any of that reading. First, much published conversion advice was developed
on high-arousal retail, and transfers badly to a calm, considered, high-trust purchase — this pack names that
dial constantly for exactly that reason. Second, a single study is a single study: prefer findings that have
survived replication, and treat any effect size quoted without its conditions as a story rather than a fact.

## Licence and sharing

This pack is original work and is safe to share, fork, and adapt. If you build on it, keep the honesty rails.
They are not decoration — they are the reason the tactics stay usable on a brand that has to survive its own
customers.

- [ ] Every idea borrowed from the bodies of work above is re-expressed in operational wording and credited by school of thought.
